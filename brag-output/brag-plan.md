# Brag Plan: SKYFOLD: The Last Flight

Two YouTube cuts from one shoot: a **90-second landscape trailer** (1920×1080) and a **60-second vertical cut** for Shorts (1080×1920). The user asked for both, with narration in several voices, motion graphics, and an end card reading "made by Awaiz Ahmed" (not Team Kanban). Their explicit length request overrides /brag's usual 15–25 s window.

## What is this app?
A hobby puzzle-adventure for Windows, playable on a laptop or in PC VR on Meta Quest 3 through Link. You repair a storm-broken coastal city by placing road and bridge pieces into a 1:160 miniature on a plinth. Then you "fold" down into the full-size city, drive the courier car across the bridge you just built, power the airfield, light the runway, and clear the last flight before the storm arrives.

## The angle
**One city, two scales.** The game's real magic is the cut between the model in your hands and the real street. Change one, and you change the other. The trailer is built on that match cut: macro tilt-shift shots of the miniature hard-cut to the same spot at full scale, so the viewer feels the fold themselves. The story then escalates like a disaster film: the broken causeway, the race across the city, the storm rolling in, the final call, and the takeoff into the storm.

## Hook (first 3 seconds)
Black. A crackle of radio static. Noor's voice: *"This is Noor. A storm is coming... and it has broken our roads."* Fade up on an extreme tilt-shift close-up of the tiny city: the broken bridge stub, the channel glinting. It reads as a real city until the camera pulls back and it is sitting on a pearl plinth on a cliff-top terrace. The viewer stays to find out which one is real.

## Key moments (the middle)
- **The placement.** A macro shot of a bridge piece hovering over the glowing socket. The wrong rotation flashes coral with the game's real line *"Not quite. The east end points into a building. Turn it a quarter."* (shown as a UI chip). It turns, drops, and snaps with a teal check.
- **The rise.** A hard cut to full scale, low over the water, as the real bridge rises out of the sea and locks in. This is the hero beat, with Noor's real line *"This model is the real city below us. Change one, and you change the other."*
- **The fold and the drive.** A swoop from the sky down to street level, the glowing power cell dropped into the courier car's cradle, then chase, side and low passes as the car crosses the bridge we just watched rise. Traffic pulls over.
- **The storm and the last flight.** A time-lapse of the storm bank rolling in, the substation lighting up, runway beacons switching on in sequence, a tower announcement (*"Final call. The last flight out of Nacre Bay is now boarding."*), the departure lever thrown, then the takeoff in the rain: a side-on roll, the lift, and a chase climb into the storm.

## Outro / punchline
The SKYFOLD wordmark folds in like paper, then "THE LAST FLIGHT". After that come two platform badges, **Play in VR (Meta Quest 3 via Link)** and **or on your laptop**, and the closing card **MADE BY AWAIZ AHMED**, with a small "Captured in engine" line.

## User flow worth showing
1. **Repair:** grab a piece in the Atelier, turn it, snap it into a socket. The real bridge rises.
2. **Live it:** fold down, carry the power cell to the car, drive across your own bridge to power the airfield.
3. **Pay it off:** light the runway, let passengers board, pull the lever, and watch the last flight take off before the storm.

## Tone
- **Preset:** cinematic.
- **Creative direction:** "a warm-coastal disaster-movie trailer for a tiny city", with big-screen gravity applied to a hand-sized world.
- **Interpretation:** wide establishing shots and macro close-ups, slow confident moves, hard cuts on impacts, and big letter-spaced uppercase type. Narration is short declarative trailer lines. Restraint in the first act, escalation through the storm, release at the takeoff.

## Audio direction
- **Role:** cinematic support with narration. The music carries the arc, and SFX mark every physical action.
- **Music:** the game's own soundtrack, edited into one bed per format:
  - Landscape: `music_menu_loop` (calm wonder) for Act I.
  - It is hit by the bridge-rise impact, then drops into `music_drive_loop` (112 BPM) for the drive.
  - It crossfades into `music_storm_loop` (tension) at the storm.
  - It hands off to `music_finale` so its climb lands on the lift-off, and rings out under the title and end card.
  - Vertical: the same order, compressed.
- **Music treatment:**
  - Ducked to about 0.15 under every voice line.
  - Rises between lines.
  - A short silence (about 0.5 s) before the bridge rises and before the title.
  - The finale is not ducked at the lift-off.
- **Music cue guidance:** the bundled presets don't apply (custom tracks). Cues come from the game music's known tempos and bar grids (72 / 112 / 90 / 96 BPM, bar-aligned loops). Lock 2–3 strong moments: the rise impact, the lift-off, and the title slam. Detect the exact strongest beats at composition time with `npx hyperframes beats`.
- **Audio-reactive treatment:** subtle. The music's low end makes the title and end-card glow breathe. No visualizers.
- **SFX posture:** moderate and motion-matched. Every one is the game's real sound:
  - fold whoosh on transitions
  - socket reject buzz and snap
  - bridge assemble boom
  - cell insert
  - car start and pass-bys
  - substation hum
  - beacon ticks, one per light group
  - lever clunk
  - distant thunder
  - plane spool-up and takeoff roar
- **Restraint rule:** never stack an SFX under a voice line's key word, and no UI clicks on cinematic shots.

## Storyboard: landscape (90 s)

### Scene 1: Cold open: "A storm is coming" (0:00–0:07, 7 s)
- **On screen:** black, then a slow fade up on A1 (tilt-shift miniature). Lower-left micro label: "NACRE BAY · 18:42" in tracking type.
- **Sequential/interaction:** the label types on.
- **Audio intent:** unease and wonder: radio static, Noor's line 1 radio-filtered, a soft coastal ambience bed.
- **Audio-coupled idea:** a static crackle in and out around the radio line.
- **Music:** `music_menu_loop` fades in under Noor.
- **Transition:** soft → Scene 2.

### Scene 2: "A city that fits in your hands" (0:07–0:14, 7 s)
- **On screen:** A2 pull-back reveal. Big centered type over the widening frame: "ONE STORM." → "ONE LAST FLIGHT." → "ONE CITY IN YOUR HANDS." Each holds at least 1.2 s, slamming in on the narrator's beats.
- **Sequential/interaction:** three lines, one per narrator phrase.
- **Audio intent:** awe as the scale flips; the narrator's line 2.
- **Audio-coupled idea:** a soft impact on each line.
- **Transition:** clean → Scene 3.

### Scene 3: The placement (0:14–0:21, 7 s)
- **On screen:** A3 macro.
  - The wrong rotation flashes coral, and a real-copy UI chip appears: "Not quite. The east end points into a building. Turn it a quarter."
  - The piece turns, drops and snaps; a teal "✓".
  - Caption: "HOLD THE CITY."
- **Sequential/interaction:** a simulated placement (reject, turn, accept).
- **Audio intent:** tactile and satisfying. Noor's line 3.
- **Audio-coupled idea:** reject buzz on the flash, rotate tick, snap on accept.
- **Transition:** hard cut (match cut) → Scene 4.

### Scene 4: The rise (0:21–0:28, 7 s)

## As built (what changed from the storyboard, and why)
- **Reject chip copy, corrected to the game.** For the east crossing the game's real hint is "Not quite. The east end runs into the water. Turn it a quarter." The capture logs it (`A3 wrong-way hint`), so the chip quotes that line instead of the "points into a building" variant. After the turn, the chip shows the game's own "Let go to set it in place."
- **Gameplay captions became an objective tracker.** Each gameplay beat is a HUD card styled like the game's panels, checked off when the action lands on screen: 01 HOLD THE CITY, 02 FOLD DOWN, 03 CARRY THE POWER, 04 DRIVE THE ROADS YOU BUILT, 05 POWER THE AIRFIELD, 06 LIGHT THE RUNWAY, 07 SEND THE LAST FLIGHT. "Send the last flight" is the Episode 3 briefing's wording. Big statement type is kept for the dramatic beats: ONE STORM / ONE LAST FLIGHT / ONE CITY IN YOUR HANDS, CHANGE ONE / CHANGE THE OTHER, THE STORM WON'T WAIT, GET THEM HOME.
- **Speakers are labelled.** Noor gets a radio badge with her in-game portrait ("City engineer · radio"); the tower gets a PA badge ("Nacre Tower · Airfield announcement").
- **Opening label** is "NACRE BAY · STORM WARNING" (no invented clock time).
- **Power beat:** B6 is a close-up of the cell going into the substation socket. B7 replaces the substation arc: the parked aircraft wakes up (cabin windows, nav lights, beacon, props), because the street-lamp change did not read on camera.
- **Takeoff:** C5 is head-on from the far end of the runway; the aircraft lifts and passes beside the lens. C6 is the chase climb into the storm.
- **Runway beacons:** C2 looks down the runway from above its west end, and both beacon rows light in one sweep racing away (the game lights its three groups side by side).
- **Voice lines:** Noor 1 says "This is Noor…" (written "Nuur" for the voice), Noor 6 is "Power is back on the island! Now light the runway." (from the game's "Power is back on the island"), and the vertical cut also uses narrator line 7.
- **Beat sync:** `npx hyperframes beats` found no beats in the ducked, ambient bed, so the grid comes from the score's generator (`tools/audio/make_music.py`: finale 96 BPM, beat 0 at the file start). The finale's key change (D to E major, bar 8) sits on the lift-off. The title letters start on a finale downbeat, and the subtitle lands two beats later.
- **Mix:** voice, music bed (ducked to 0.3 under every line, hushed before the bridge rises) and effects, levelled together to about -14 LUFS with true peaks under -1 dBFS.
- **Capture:** the shots are recorded from the release build by `Assets/Skyfold/Scripts/Runtime/Cinematic/CineDirector.cs` (`--cine`). It runs a fixed 30 fps clock paced to real time, supersamples 2560x1440 / 1440x2560, adds a light bloom and vignette, and pipes frames to ffmpeg. The edit lists live in `tools/trailer/edits.py`, and `tools/trailer/build_trailer.py` builds the stems and both compositions from them.
